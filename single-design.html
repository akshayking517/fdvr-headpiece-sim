This is the near-term headpiece: a tiny projector paints the image directly onto your retina instead of bouncing it off a screen and lens stack, and a room-scale transmitter powers it over the air. Same envelope as the essay's cortical-writing endgame — same 40 W room node, same wireless link — just a different last component. Writing straight to the visual cortex, bypassing the eye entirely, isn't buildable yet; this is. Set the parts and watch two limits at once: how much power the room can deliver, and how much heat the scalp can shed.

Model: head-side load is receive power (delivered bit rate × energy per bit) plus projector power (light ÷ wall-plug efficiency) plus tracking plus conversion loss. Room delivery is field strength × capture area × harvesting efficiency. The thermal ceiling is 94.8 W/m² of contact (84.8 W/m² if the device blocks all heat loss to room air), from a four-layer tissue heat-transfer model held to a 1 °C rise.
